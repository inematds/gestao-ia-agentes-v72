Lesson 8 · 20 min

Project: the hired agent + What changed

A new person only starts after a signed contract, a described role and a defined boss. Agents too. The project for this course is the hiring dossier: the pieces from lessons 1 to 7, put together, run through a rubric and signed by someone who didn't write them. At the end, what changed in the market, with date and source.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1What goes into the hiring dossier and in what order
  2. 2Apply the rubric, including the items that fail on their own
  3. 3Who signs the hire (and why it is not the person who built it)
  4. 4What changed through October 2026, with date and source

Goal: build the agent's hiring dossier and approve it with the rubric before it starts working, with a signature from someone who did not write it.

02 · From draft to signature

Four steps. Hora's dossier is the practice run; your agent's dossier is the one that counts.

1. Gather the pieces

Put the pieces from lessons 1 to 7 in a single folder, in the order of the diagram, with a one-page cover: agent, process, version, owner, date and the index.

If a piece changed after it was written (the matrix gained a row in lesson 7, for example), update the version. A dossier with pieces from different versions is the first thing the rubric catches.

At HorizonteFolder "Hora · dossier v2.0": cover, job sheet, restocking delegation, constitution with 8 test cases, matrix with the checklist for the promoted action, supervision routine and the design with the collections agent.

2. Run it through the rubric

The rubric adds up to 100 points across six blocks. You pass with 75 or more and every must-have checked. One missing must-have fails it, even with 95 points.

The must-haves are the ones that prevent the big damage: a named owner, nothing irreversible in green, a cap in numbers wherever money is involved, every exception with a destination and a deadline, a written demotion criterion, and fictional or anonymized data labeled as such.

Panel testRun the panel prompt (template alongside). It points out gaps; the grade is decided by you and by whoever signs.

3. Who signs

Whoever put the dossier together does not approve the hiring, for the same reason that whoever enters a payment does not approve it. The person who signs reads the cover, checks the must-haves and asks about anything they did not understand.

IT confirms in writing that the safeguards requested (discount cap, maximum amount, logging, off switch) exist in the tool, and not just in the text.

At HorizontePatrícia puts it together. Rafael confirms the safeguards. Carlos reads, asks questions and signs. Juliana gets the supervision routine because she is Patrícia's backup.

4. Review date

The dossier goes out with a review date: 30 days after hiring, or sooner if there is a serious failure, or a change of model, tool or vendor.

At the review, the pieces go back through the rubric. This is how the agent moves up a level without anyone having to remember what the rule used to be.

RuleIf the model or the tool changes: the affected actions go back to yellow and the test cases run again.

04 · What changed · October 2026

Quarterly panel, replaced at each course review. Each item has a date, a source and the type of claim. The products are examples: the method in lessons 1 to 7 does not depend on any of them.

1. Agents became a product

OpenAI: Workspace Agents, which replace Custom GPTs, are now billed per token (07/06/2026); ChatGPT Work delivers documents, spreadsheets and slides (07/09/2026). The AgentKit Agent Builder will be shut down on 11/30/2026.

Anthropic: Managed Agents and, in Claude Code, background computer use (09/2026). Microsoft: Agent 365, announced at Build (06/2026). Sources: each vendor's release notes and announcements.

For the dossierProducts get discontinued and change price within months. The dossier records which tool the agent uses and what happens if it changes: actions go back to yellow and the tests run again.

2. How management is changing

Microsoft, Work Trend Index 2025: the "agent boss," a human manager of agents, and the metric of the ratio between humans and agents. WTI 2026 (05/2026, 20,000 people in 10 countries): when the manager uses AI in front of the team, perceived value goes up 17 points (vendor survey).

Deloitte, Tech Trends 2026: graduated autonomy and agent integration. Gartner, forecast from 10/2024: by the end of 2026, 1 in 5 organizations will use AI to cut more than half of middle management.

ReadingTaking a layer off the org chart does not eliminate its work: coordinating, reviewing, training people. That is the work the dossier and the exception queue organize.

3. Brazil and the world

GFT, Tech Trends 2026 LATAM, with data from BCG: 18% of Brazilian companies integrate agents, versus 13% worldwide. ABES and IDC (06/15/2026, 103 executives): 40% already invest in agents and 33% plan to within 12 months. Gartner (2026): only 17% of companies have agents in use.

Deloitte (09/17/2026): 27% of Brazilian companies have mature AI governance. Okta (2026): nearly two-thirds apply weaker controls to agents than to people.

Why the numbers don't match"Integrate," "invest" and "in use" measure different things, with different samples. Compare the definition before you compare the percentage.

4. How to read these reports

Who pays, and what do they sell? Microsoft and Okta sell the solution to the problem they measure; consultancies sell the transformation they describe. How did they measure? A survey with a sample, self-reported experience, or a forecast? What is a forecast?

An analysis from The Microsoft Cloud Blog (05/2026) notes that the WTI 2026 numbers should not support a business case on their own. Use a report as a map, never as proof of your case.

Next review of this panelJanuary 2027, with the next quarter's overview. The method (lessons 1 to 7) does not change; products and numbers do.
