Lesson 6 · 20 min

One agent or a team

Carlos liked Hora and already wants another agent, for collections. Maybe a third one to check the first two. Sometimes that's right; sometimes it's just a bigger problem with more places to go wrong. In this lesson you decide when the work calls for more than one agent, how they hand work to each other, and where the team stops.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Take fixed rules out of the agent
  2. 2Recognize the three signs that it's time for a team
  3. 3Write the handoff contract between agents
  4. 4Decide series or parallel, and set limits on attempts, time, and cost

Goal: decide when to split the work across several agents, in series or in parallel, with a handoff contract and limits on attempts, time, and cost.

01 · The decision ladder

Click each block in the diagram to see what it does.

"Intelligence in sequence, automation in parallel": rule from the Execution Architect (INEMA). The three signs come from the Super-Agents in practice course.

02 · Horizonte Distribuidora wants to grow the team

Carlos's request: an agent to remind customers about invoices coming due. Four decisions.

1. First, take the rule out of the agent

Someone suggested a "checking agent" to review Hora's orders. But checking list price, discount ceiling, amounts above R$ 5 mil and customer records is a fixed rule: the same input always gives the same answer.

That goes into the order tool, as Rafael already did after lesson 3. An agent to do math is cost and risk for no reason.

DecisionOrder checking: a rule in the tool, not an agent.

2. Is collections another agent?

Collections needs to read accounts receivable in the ERP, data that Hora should not see. It has a different tone, different rules (never threaten, never renegotiate alone) and a different owner, finance.

This is the second sign: permissions and rules that can't live in the same agent. Here the split is justified. The collections agent gets its own job sheet, constitution and matrix, all starting at yellow.

Design (fictional proposal)Hora (sales) → invoiced order in the ERP → collections agent (reminder) → finance decides on renegotiation.

3. Write the handoff

Every handoff between agents has a contract: the trigger, what gets passed, the evidence that goes with it, what the receiver cannot do, the deadline and who it goes back to.

Pass the minimum needed. The collections agent receives the invoice number, due date and authorized contact, not the whole sales conversation. Less data moving around means less risk and less paid context.

Golden ruleWhoever receives it reads the record of what was done and does not start from zero, nor ask the customer what they have already said.

4. Set limits and stops

A team without limits gets stuck in a loop: one agent sends it back to the other, which sends it back again, and the bill grows. Write it down: at most two attempts per task, one handback between agents and then a person; a deadline per step; a monthly budget for the team.

McKinsey describes organizations with small teams per outcome, with 2 to 5 people supervising 50 to 100 agents. This is the consultancy's own stated experience, not a measured study. The unit is the outcome, not the number of agents.

At Horizonte (fictional values)Reminder: at most 2 per invoice. Tool error: 2 attempts, then it alerts finance. Team budget: monthly ceiling in writing; past 80%, Patrícia is notified.

Simulation: illustrative example

Horizonte, fictional study company · owners: Patrícia (sales) and finance (collections).

  • Order check · rule in the tool: price, cap, amount, customer record.
  • Hora · sales agent · logs restock orders.
  • Collections agent · kept separate because of the permissions signal: reads accounts receivable, sends an approved reminder; never renegotiates.
  • People · Patrícia approves orders above R$ 5 mil and discounts from 5% to 10%; finance decides renegotiation.
  • Series: order → approval → picking → invoicing → reminder. Parallel: reminders for different invoices, as an automatic routine.
  • Trigger: invoice issued in the ERP.
  • Passes: invoice number, amount, due date, contact authorized for collections.
  • Does not pass: the sales conversation, negotiated discounts, other contacts.
  • Collections cannot: renegotiate, give a discount, threaten, or collect before the due date.
  • Missing data: send it back once to Juliana; the second time, to finance.
  • Limit: 2 reminders per invoice.

04 · Practice

Draw Horizonte's team, then yours. This is on paper: no new agent gets turned on today.

1 · The team from order to collections

30 min

With the model, draw the team for Horizonte's process, from the WhatsApp order to the collections reminder. For each piece, say whether it is a rule, an agent or a person, and why. Write the handoff contract from Hora to the collections agent without looking at the example; then compare.

Done when: the drawing has at least one piece you decided is NOT an agent, each agent has the signal that justifies it existing separately, the handoff contract states what passes and what does not, and there are limits on attempts, send-backs, deadline and budget.

2 · Your team, or your agent on its own

25 min

Take your process and climb the ladder: which parts are fixed steps? Is one agent enough? Does any of the three signals show up? If so, draw the team and write the contract for one handoff. If not, write in two lines why one agent is enough for now.

Done when: you have the decision in writing (one agent or a team) with the signal that justifies it, and, if it is a team, the drawing and a handoff contract with limits.

06 · Summary

A rule is not an agentWhatever has a fixed answer goes into the tool.
Three signalsThe desk that overflows, different permissions, independent check. Without them, one agent is enough.
A contract on every arrowTrigger, what passes and what does not, prohibitions, deadline, send-back.
Team limitsAttempts, send-backs, deadline, budget and one button that stops everyone.
