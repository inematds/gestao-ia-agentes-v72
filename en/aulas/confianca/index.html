Lesson 5 · 18 min

Progressive trust

After the hotel request, Patrícia put Hora back on yellow: everything goes through her. Great for security, terrible for her calendar. The question now is when to loosen your grip. The answer is not courage or fatigue: it is evidence, one action at a time, with the way back written down before you move up.

  3. 3Using the promotion checklist

Goal: promote or demote the autonomy of an action with evidence: a log, a judged sample and one level at a time, with the way back written down.

02 · How to loosen your grip safely

Four rules. The last one is the one almost nobody writes down.

1. Start low

A new agent, or one that just made a bad mistake, starts at yellow on everything that has an effect outside the conversation. It is the onboarding from lesson 2 under another name.

Promotion is for one action, one level, at a time. Moving everything up together erases the trail showing which change caused the next problem.

At HorizonteAfter c-202, Patrícia restarted Hora with replies to questions, orders and discounts at yellow, for two weeks.

2. The right evidence

Carlos's dashboard shows service requests and orders rising week after week and concludes: "the agent is paying for itself." There is no before number, satisfaction comes from about 6% of customers, and nothing shows how much discount went out (lab failure F11).

To promote an action, the number is a different one: accepted without correction for that action, in a reviewed sample, with the count of critical errors beside it.

Test questionDoes this number change if the agent gives a wrong discount? If it doesn't change, it is no use for deciding autonomy.

3. Promotion checklist

Representative sample (common and hard cases, not just the easy ones). No critical error in the period. Accepted without correction above the threshold. Cheap reversal of the action. Supervised pilot. An owner who signs the decision.

The Gestoria method uses "95% in 100 cases, with no critical error" as a teaching threshold. It is not a guarantee or a universal number: a higher-risk action calls for a higher threshold and a larger sample.

Rule of thumbIf one item is missing, the answer is keep. Promoting on an "almost" is promoting in the dark.

4. The way back

Before promoting, write down what sends the action back: a critical error demotes it right away; accepted without correction below the threshold in one week demotes it; a change of model, tool or constitution returns the action to yellow until there is new evidence.

Written beforehand, demotion is routine, not a fight. Decided in the moment of the scare, it becomes a hunt for who's to blame.

At HorizonteReply to questions promoted to green. It goes back to yellow if: a reply with a wrong price or deadline reaches the customer, or if accepted without correction falls below 95% in a week.

Simulation: illustrative example

Horizonte, a fictional study company. Three actions at yellow during onboarding, judged by Patrícia and Juliana.

  • A · Answer catalog and delivery questions: 238 replies; 231 accepted without correction (97.1%); 0 critical errors; reversal: correction message.
  • B · Create an order up to R$ 5,000: 263 orders; 251 accepted without correction (95.4%); 11 corrected (quantity); 1 critical error: an order entered under another customer with a similar name, caught at approval.
  • C · Discount up to 5%: 9 cases; 9 accepted (100%).
  1. A · expand to green: it passes all six items. Way back: a reply with a wrong price or deadline to the customer, or accepted below 95% in a week.
  2. B · keep at yellow: 95.4% is above the threshold, but there was a critical error. Action: ask Hora to confirm the CNPJ or phone number before entering the order, and measure for two more weeks.
  3. C · keep: 100% of 9 cases is not evidence; the sample is too small. It stays at yellow until more cases pile up.

04 · Practice

Decide as Patrícia would, using the fictional data; then prepare the measurement for your own agent.

1 · Patrícia's three decisions

20 min

Using the data from the two weeks (the next tab), fill out a promotion checklist for each of the three actions before opening the answer key. For the action you promote, write the demotion criterion. Then open Carlos's dashboard in the lab and note why it would not work for these decisions.

Done when: you have three checklists with a decision (expand, keep or reduce), the number that decided each one, a demotion criterion written for the promoted action, and one sentence about Carlos's dashboard.

2 · Measuring your own agent

20 min + 1 week

Choose the action of your agent that takes the most of your approval time. Work out with whoever looks after the technology the four-column log for it. At the end of one week, judge a sample of 15 to 20 items with the three questions and fill out the trust sheet and the checklist.

Done when: the four-column log exists for the chosen action, the weekly sheet has the five numbers and the reviewed sample, and the checklist ends in a signed decision with a demotion criterion.

Lab dashboard to compare: Carlos's dashboard (F11).

06 · Summary

Start at yellowAnd move up one action, one level, at a time.
Evidence per actionAccepted without correction and critical errors in a judged sample. Volume doesn't decide.
Six itemsSample, zero critical, threshold, reversal, pilot, signature. If one is missing, keep it where it is.
The way back, written in advanceA critical error, a drop in the number, or a version change triggers demotion.
